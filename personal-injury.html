What to do in the first 72 hours

  1. 1
    Get medical care and keep the records. ER notes, imaging, and follow-up visits are the file. Gaps in treatment are what insurers argue later.
  2. 2
    Photograph the vehicles, the scene, and visible injuries. Do not rely on the other driver or the carrier to preserve that.
  3. 3
    Identify every policy. The at-fault driver, your own liability, PIP or med-pay if any, and UM/UIM. Limits matter more than the first adjuster call.
  4. 4
    Do not give a recorded statement or sign a broad release until you know what you are settling. See our post-crash practice note.

Texas wrecks vs. Oklahoma wrecks

Both states are at-fault systems for most car-accident injury claims: the person who caused the crash (and that person’s insurer) is the starting point, not a no-fault PIP suit. Comparative-negligence rules still reduce or bar a recovery if the injured person is found partly at fault. Many injury lawsuits in both states are subject to a two-year limitations period, but insurance notice clauses, claims involving minors, and governmental defendants can change that date. Treat those rules as a reason to calendar the crash, not as a DIY filing deadline.

The crash location usually controls the claim, even if you live in the other state. A Dallas resident hurt on I-35 in Oklahoma still has an Oklahoma fact pattern. An Edmond resident hurt on I-35E in Dallas still has a Texas fact pattern. That is why this practice is built around both offices.

Coverage we map on every file

A crash is rarely one policy. Before anyone talks “case value,” we identify who actually pays:

  • The at-fault driver’s liability limits
  • Your own liability, PIP, or med-pay, if the policy carries it
  • Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage on your household policies
  • Rideshare, company-car, or commercial layers when those vehicles are in the wreck
  • Health-insurance liens and reimbursement claims that come out of a settlement

That map is why we ask for the crash city, the other driver’s insurer, and whether anyone has already requested a recorded statement. The 72-hour practice note walks through what to gather before that call.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle personal injury cases in both Texas and Oklahoma?
Yes. We review car-accident injury claims for people hurt in either state, from the Dallas and Edmond offices.
Do I pay an attorney fee if there is no recovery?
In appropriate cases, no attorney fee is charged if there is no recovery. Expenses are separate. That is confirmed in a written fee agreement before work begins.
Should I sign the first settlement offer?
Usually not before a review. Early offers often arrive before future care, lost time, and coverage limits are known.
How long do I have to file?
Many Texas and Oklahoma injury claims run on a two-year clock, but the real date depends on the facts. Call if the crash is approaching a year old.
