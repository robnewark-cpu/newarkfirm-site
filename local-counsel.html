What this page is for

Short answer: Lead counsel outside our offices often needs a lawyer who can walk into the courthouse, sit a deposition, or sign a local filing. We will consider that role in the jurisdictions listed below. We will also decline when the docket, the conflict check, or the work is a poor fit. That is the point of case-by-case.

Primary offices remain Dallas, Texas and Edmond, Oklahoma. That is where files are run. Local-counsel work in other licensed courts is coordinated from those offices, not from a storefront in every state.

What local counsel usually means on our files

  • Court appearances, status conferences, and hearings where lead counsel cannot be on the ground
  • Deposition coverage and local scheduling with court reporters and witnesses
  • Filing, service, and local-rule compliance when the court expects a licensed lawyer of record
  • Coordination with lead counsel on strategy — we do not take over the case unless that is the written engagement

Pro hac vice still has to be available under the court’s rules when the lead firm is not admitted. Local counsel does not magically admit an out-of-state lawyer. We will say so if the motion is not a fit.

What we are not doing on this page

  • Opening a general consumer practice in Alaska, Wisconsin, or South Dakota
  • Advertising state-court work in Colorado, New Mexico, or Arkansas
  • Promising we will take every local-counsel request that comes in
Fee terms for local-counsel work are set in a written engagement — hourly, flat, or another structure the matter supports.
