Wind & Hail Claim Denials: What Property Owners Should Know

A denial or lowball estimate is often the start of the claim, not the end of it.

This article is general information, not legal advice, and does not create an attorney-client relationship.

Common reasons insurers give

  • Wear and tear, deterioration, or “pre-existing damage.”
  • An estimate that covers only a fraction of the actual repair cost.
  • A dispute over whether a particular storm caused the loss.
  • Delay: the claim stays open while the roof, interior, or business interruption gets worse.

Some of those positions are legitimate. Many are not. The difference is usually in the policy language, the photos, the weather data, and whether the carrier followed its own claim-handling duties.

Deadlines still control the file

Most policies impose notice, suit-limitation, and appraisal deadlines. Waiting for a second adjuster visit can quietly burn those clocks. If the estimate is wrong, appraisal or a formal dispute may be available — but only if it is invoked in time.

What to gather before a review

Policy, declaration pages, the estimate, denial letter, photos, invoices, and a timeline of every call with the carrier. That package is what we use on our Texas and Oklahoma wind and hail claim pages.
